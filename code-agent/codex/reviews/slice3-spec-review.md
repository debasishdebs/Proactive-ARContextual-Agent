1. **Critical — The helper “sandbox” permits laptop compromise.** Slice 3 §§4.4, 9 execute arbitrary Python, generated Kotlin tests, and CLIs as the laptop user. A scratch directory/worktree and timeout do not restrict access to credentials, other repositories, network, or child processes. Read-only patch review happens *after* this execution.  
   **Fix:** execute untrusted jobs in an actual isolation boundary with restricted mounts, credentials, network, resources, and process-tree termination. Give promotion access only to the required repository snapshot; keep helper credentials outside that boundary.

2. **Important — “Capture now is exempt from everything” contradicts the privacy and safety invariants.** §3.4 can be read to bypass wear, pause, thermal protection, Forget/wipe fences, and LED requirements in §§3.1–2. A3 §5 exempts forced captures specifically from the rate-cap rule.  
   **Fix:** enumerate the exemptions—such as novelty and rate limits—and explicitly preserve privacy fences, camera ownership, LED, and mandatory thermal limits. Add precedence tests for forced capture during wipe and unsafe heat.

3. **Important — Plan acknowledgements cannot identify the operation being acknowledged.** §5.3 sends `publishId`, mode, timestamps, and hash, but acknowledges only hash. SHADOW and LIVE of the same plan share a hash; duplicate publishes and `null` rollbacks are also ambiguous. No monotonic ordering or stale-ack rule is specified.  
   **Fix:** use a durable command generation plus session epoch; echo generation, publish ID, mode, and effective hash in the ack. Specify idempotency, stale-message rejection, restart behavior, and unsupported-capability handling.

4. **Important — Publishing is transactional only inside Room, not across execution domains.** §7 inserts two rows atomically, but phone fusion, glasses capture, and the ASYNC interpreter activate separately. Glasses rejection or disconnection can leave a mixed configuration. A local auto-revert does not clearly deactivate the phone half or prevent `onReady()` from reinstalling the rejected plan.  
   **Fix:** define desired, prepared, effective, rejected, and reverted states for each component. Coordinate activation and compensation, persist terminal reverts, and reconcile effective state on reconnect.

5. **Important — Rollback rules disagree with each other and Slice 2.** §7 says every rollback sends `planJson=null`, while claiming Slice 2 §6 stack semantics. Rolling back plan B should reveal still-active plan A; sending `null` selects settings instead. “Use settings” also cannot reliably mean “undo the newest row of any kind” when that row is a prompt publish.  
   **Fix:** distinguish **undo latest publish**, **restore previous capture plan**, and **disable capture overrides/use settings**. Define full-graph publish grouping and deterministic rollback order.

6. **Important — The supposedly frozen plan depends on mutable settings.** §§1, 5.2, 7 freeze params, but omitted fields resolve against current settings. Changing an unoverridden setting changes execution without changing the plan hash or invalidating its replay. Separately, `{}` cannot both reproduce MOTION settings exactly and enforce §3’s mandatory SETTLE behavior.  
   **Fix:** distinguish a settings-delegation sentinel from a published plan. Resolve and hash the effective configuration at publication, or include a settings revision and require replay after relevant changes. State the identity-plan exception explicitly.

7. **Important — The battery gate permits plans that violate the binding four-hour requirement.** §§1, 6.2 permit publishing unless drain exceeds **both** baseline ×1.10 and 0.42 %/min. Against the stated 0.49 baseline, a 0.53 plan passes despite providing only about 3.1 hours. Verification is not a prerequisite for LIVE.  
   **Fix:** reserve unverified plans for SHADOW or an explicitly bounded trial. Require measured acceptance against the four-hour target before unrestricted LIVE, or explicitly revise the binding requirement.

8. **Important — The drain model is not sufficiently identifiable as specified.** §6.2 fits three slopes from the stated A3 anchor and A2 second point: that supplies only one independent delta equation. More observations may exist, but the spec requires neither sufficient rank nor independent variation. “± error” has no defined calculation. §6.3 also starts one recording across SHADOW and LIVE without requiring phase-separated imports.  
   **Fix:** require a full-rank calibration dataset or use a simpler model; define uncertainty and extrapolation limits. Store plan/settings hashes, mode, and phase boundaries, and fit only the relevant LIVE interval.

9. **Important — Replay cannot support the promised exact keep/skip parity.** §§6.1–2 retain keyframes, then substitute the nearest frame within two seconds for a simulated open. Rejected candidates, blur selection, and views at different capture times are missing. Even identity replay can lack the samples that caused real duplicate/quality skips. SHADOW cannot observe counterfactual keeps either. This conflicts with §10’s bit-identical sequences and ±15% comparison.  
   **Fix:** separate exact decision replay from approximate visual replay. Record sufficient candidate metadata and initial state for exact observed-path tests; report counterfactual visual outcomes as unknown, with coverage and bounds.

10. **Important — Replay does not actually evaluate several tunable parameters.** §6.2 selects policy using the recorded hub state, while §§5.2, 8 allow changing fusion parameters. Reusing committed states bypasses those changes. Altered capture timing also changes available intent evidence, which feeds back into fusion.  
    **Fix:** replay timestamped evidence through `ContextFusion` with the proposed parameters. Model intent availability explicitly; restrict unsupported sweeps and mark unobservable feedback outcomes rather than treating recorded states as counterfactual truth.

11. **Important — The no-pause invariant does not follow from the proposed restrictions.** §§3.5, 4.3 permit a guard equivalent to `false`, or `boutRad >` every attainable value. Because guards run on timer checks too, an accepted plan can suppress all automatic captures indefinitely. `capPerMin ≥1` is an upper allowance, not a liveness guarantee.  
    **Fix:** define the promise as bounded capture opportunities when otherwise eligible, and reserve a timer path that arbitrary guards cannot suppress. Otherwise weaken the invariant and its validation claim.

12. **Important — The walking guarantee is incompatible with the inherited gate and editable policy.** §3 describes walking priority/veto as unconditional. Wave B §§6.1, 9.1 deliberately exempt SPEED evidence from the veto, and the local veto returns UNKNOWN rather than WALKING. Slice 3 allows UNKNOWN and WALKING policy rows to reduce capture to 1/min. Thus vetoing a false vehicle state need not restore full capture.  
    **Fix:** document the SPEED exception and define the exact effective walking policy. If Wave B’s full-rate promise remains binding, prevent restrictive fallback rows from defeating it.

13. **Important — The claimed RT cost ceiling is not established.** §4.3 counts six ×32 AST operations but excludes window aggregation and table lookup work. §8 permits zero check gaps, without specifying scheduler floors or event coalescing. Arithmetic overflow can also produce infinity/NaN, for which “clamp then apply invariants” is insufficiently defined.  
    **Fix:** specify bounded window/lookup implementations, memory limits, event-rate enforcement, and finite typed results. Test maximum-cost programs and overflow behavior on the glasses; fail safely on evaluator errors.

14. **Important — The advertised graph cannot be expressed under the domain rules.** §§2, 4.1 forbid cross-domain edges except the capture boundary, but §8 gives `hub.context` RT_PHONE branches feeding a capture policy in RT_GLASSES. The required glasses-side signal source/receiver is not defined. The inherited MVP validator also requires all nodes to be reachable from `input.photos`, excluding RT roots.  
    **Fix:** specify separate rooted domain graphs and explicit signal bindings, including their ports and validation rules. Add an end-to-end validation golden for the exact §1 example.

15. **Important — Slice 2’s interpreter rejects the new live nodes, and their side effects lack retry semantics.** Slice 2 §5 admits only photos/prompt/switch before extraction and trust/critic/write afterward. Slice 3 adds event, detector, embedding, table, and tool executors without replacing that grammar. If side effects happen before a later extraction failure, Slice 2’s whole-batch fallback/retry can repeat proposals or evidence writes.  
    **Fix:** define the revised live shape and executor contracts. Buffer persistent effects behind the appropriate fences and use stable idempotency keys across fallback, retries, and restarts.

16. **Important — Intent-sampler ownership is contradictory and can disable sensing.** §8 S14 says hub cadence uses photos selected by `IntentSampler`, then says that sampler stands down whenever a published graph has `event.hub_intent`. It does not identify the remaining scheduler. “Published” also includes SHADOW, which only samples one in four batches under Slice 2 §5. Wave B §§5, 7 requires opt-in, one in-flight call, frame-time evidence, and freshness limits; these are not preserved explicitly.  
    **Fix:** keep one shared admission scheduler and lease classification ownership only to a healthy LIVE interpreter. Preserve source switches, freshness, and call limits; make SHADOW evidence read-only and restore ownership on fallback/rollback.

17. **Important — Detection output will lose data under the proposed contract.** §8b’s prompt returns object counts and people counts/activities, but `frame_detection` has only label, score, and optional box. The recommended merged extraction schema has no specified path through `ExtractedBatch` to this table. Multiple instances, retries, and model changes also have no defined identity/replacement rule.  
    **Fix:** define a normalized detection result and persistence adapter for both separate and merged prompts. Preserve supported counts/activities explicitly and specify provenance, instance identity, and idempotent replacement.

18. **Important — Forget does not remove all newly retained derivatives.** §11 deletes trace events and labels for a time range, but does not redact/delete the corresponding sealed IMU file. Replay/sweep results and derived summaries have no invalidation rule. Deleting a starred frame removes only its INTENT labels, leaving an imported MOMENT label. Pending helper jobs or late detector results can recreate deleted derivatives unless separately fenced.  
    **Fix:** specify a provenance-based deletion graph covering files, labels, results, caches, and jobs. Apply wipe generations/tombstones at every result commit and transfer acceptance, not just the memory-save tail.

19. **Important — Helper pairing and revocation leave trust gaps.** §§4.4, 9 pin whichever certificate is encountered first, without Slice 2’s explicit fingerprint comparison. A browser’s existing authority also gains the ability to submit laptop execution jobs without a defined capability boundary. Disconnect/sign-out does not specify cancellation of already leased jobs or deletion of laptop copies.  
    **Fix:** authenticate the phone fingerprint out of band before pairing; explicitly grant execution/promotion capabilities. Define leases, job ownership, revocation, process cancellation, and bounded retention of inputs, outputs, and patches.

20. **Important — Promotion’s acceptance oracle can be written by the same generator it evaluates.** §4.4 asks the CLI to generate both implementation and JUnit tests, then accepts passing tests. A generated test can ignore the implementation or change expected results. Finite golden cases also establish agreement only on those cases, not semantic equivalence.  
    **Fix:** use an immutable, helper-owned comparator and test adapter outside the generated patch. Verify golden hashes and reject modifications to the harness/build verification path. Report sampled agreement, add held-out/boundary cases, and retain human review.

21. **Important — Schema-1 compatibility and hash migration are underspecified.** §4.2 says old pipelines load unchanged while the hash gains `type@version`, but the old format has no per-node version. Default insertion or version normalization can change hashes for frozen versions, previous DONE runs, and active publishes, breaking exact-hash gates or silently selecting changed behavior.  
    **Fix:** define explicit legacy-version resolution and canonicalization. Preserve old immutable records/hashes, create migrated successor versions where necessary, and test upgrade fixtures containing frozen pipelines, runs, and active publishes.

22. **Important — Trace replay and transfer lack clock and integrity contracts.** §6.1 joins glasses sensor events, phone hub events, and frames by time without defining clock conversion, reboot epochs, initial state, or ordering. `pc_trace_chunk` has no specified sequence, length limits, completeness check, acknowledgement, resume behavior, or wipe generation.  
    **Fix:** version a trace manifest with clock mappings, sequence/order, settings/state snapshots, bounded compressed/decompressed sizes, checksums, and completion status. Reject incomplete traces for publish evidence and prevent delayed chunks from resurrecting deleted traces.

23. **Important — The task dependencies permit incomplete integration and migration conflicts.** §12 lets T5 compile expressions without depending on T3; T11 publish without depending on T6’s glasses application or T8’s phone application; and T16 finish without the new executor tasks. T9 owns v7, while later T17 adds `frame_detection` “in v7,” conflicting with the never-edit-existing-migrations rule once T9 lands.  
    **Fix:** add the missing execution dependencies and an integration milestone before publication. Finalize the combined schema before its migration lands, or give later additions their own next-free migration.

24. **Minor — Battery verification durations disagree.** §6.3 requires at least 20 minutes SHADOW plus 30 minutes LIVE; §10 requires a point worn for at least 60 minutes. Neither states whether 60 minutes means LIVE exposure or total recording.  
    **Fix:** specify one minimum valid LIVE interval and separate SHADOW warm-up, with identical import and checklist rules.

25. **Minor — The deferred biometric constraints omit downstream information flow.** §8b prohibits exporting embeddings and matches, but does not define whether a recognized name may enter `context_sources`, captions, facts, traces, tool arguments, or helper jobs. Converting a match to ordinary text could bypass the stated restriction. Recognition is deferred, so this is a requirement for its later spec rather than a Slice 3 implementation blocker.  
    **Fix:** define a biometric-derived data boundary and deletion provenance before registering recognition, including tests that identity results cannot reach prohibited sinks.
